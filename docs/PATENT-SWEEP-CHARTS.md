## US 8,458,680 B2: "Systems and methods for dynamically choosing a processing element for a compute kernel"

Independent claims 1 (method), 9 (system) and 17 (non-transitory medium). Their elements are the same; 9 adds "memory" and "at least one program stored in the memory", 17 recites "one or more programs ... executed by a parallel-processing computer that includes multiple types of processing elements using shared memory". One chart covers all three.

**Elements that may be ABSENT, first:** (a) "using shared memory" (E0); (b) "receiving one or more operation requests issued by an application ... at runtime" taken as call-by-call requests (E1, partly). Neither is absent in the general case; both depend on how a court reads the words, and (a) depends on the hardware Fuel runs on. Every other element is present.

## Points a lawyer would argue (the PM asked for these to be recorded)

1. **"Using shared memory" ('680 preamble, E0).** Whether the phrase limits claim 1 at all (a preamble is limiting only if the body depends on it or the claim is read to need it for antecedent basis or life and meaning), and, if it does, whether a system whose processing elements have separate memories joined by explicit copies "uses shared memory". Fuel's cross-device copies (`insert_residency_copies`, `fuel-dispatch/src/residency.rs`) are the fact pattern; unified-memory hardware is the fact pattern that cuts the other way.
2. **Call-by-call requests versus a lazy graph ('680 E1, '368 R1).** Whether "receiving one or more operation requests issued by an application ... at runtime" covers a runtime that receives ops one at a time and executes them, or also a runtime that accumulates them into a graph, plans the whole graph, and executes at realize. Fuel's design is the second; the patents' own examples (API function calls, dependent claim 7 of '680) are closer to the first. The argument is about "at runtime" and "receiving", not about whether the planning happens inside the application's process (it does).
3. **"Code reuse section" ('066 P1)** and **"previously compiled ... by the runtime system for the application" ('066 P4)**, as charted above.

### '307 claim 20 (adds a coarse scale factor)

Claim 20 additionally requires "receiving a coarse scale factor that is shared by the quantized parameters in the first multi-dimensional input tensor" and "multiplying each element of the multi-dimensional output tensor by the coarse scale factor". Fuel's k-quant super-block scale `d` is shared by the eight 32-value sub-blocks of one 256-value super-block (`BlockQ4K`, `k_quants.rs:144-149`), not by the whole tensor, and Fuel does not multiply the finished output tensor by a tensor-wide scale on these paths. **Rating: absent as worded** (a tensor-wide coarse scale), so claim 20 does not read on the GGUF paths; `fuel-ir/src/quant_scale.rs` has a `PerTensor` granularity tag, but I found no path that combines it with per-vector scales and multiplies the output, which I did not search exhaustively.

### Design-around, and why none is proposed

Per-block scaled integer dot products are how every GGUF block format works (they come from ggml, MIT; the layouts are public and GGUF files circulate), so avoiding the claims would mean dropping those formats on the CPU, which is a product decision for CireSnave with counsel, not a code change this lane can propose. Where the claims plausibly do **not** read: '307 claim 20 (tensor-wide coarse scale), '082 on one-level formats (Q8_0, Q4_0, Q5_0), and weight-only 4-bit paths that dequantize to 16 bits before multiplying. Options for the PM, not evaluated: a lawyer's review of both for validity (block-floating-point and group-wise quantization literature before the 2020-08-28 priority date is a prior-art question I did not assess; VS-Quant itself, arXiv 2102.04503, was published in 2021, after the priority date) and for whether a licence or an Nvidia pledge applies (not researched).

**Not done:** the two patents' continuations and any related applications; maintenance-fee status; Nvidia's own kernels and cuBLAS/CUTLASS (relevant to whether Fuel's use of baracuda GPU kernels adds exposure, since baracuda's kernel code was not read); the specification text (only the claims and the front page were read).
